# Subcorpus 17 — Chapter Overviews (Volume Backing Index)

This auxiliary file provides chapter-level backing-index entries for
the ethics-and-professional-standards volume draft at
`volumes/ethics-volume-draft.md`. Each chapter overview enumerates
the chapter's cards with their primary anchor sources, so a chapter-
lead paragraph in the volume that previews multiple cards can cite
this file via `[Backing card](_chapter_overviews.md#chapter-N-overview)`
and transitively inherit the per-card source markers below.

The file is auxiliary (filename starts with `_`) and is not a
deliverable card; the card linter skips it. The volume citation
checker accepts anchored backing-card links to it because each
chapter section below contains `**Source:**` markers on every
per-card paragraph; the TRANS-001 backing-card-link transitivity
check enforces one paragraph per declared card so the chapter lead
inherits the per-card sourced evidence.

## Chapter 1 Overview

The Ethics & Trust + Code of Ethics chapter establishes the trust
foundation for investment-management ethics and introduces the six
binding Code principles that operationalize the trust framework.

The ethics-and-trust material in
[`cc-ethics-and-trust.md`](cc-ethics-and-trust.md) establishes Vol.6
Reading 56's trust foundation: the principal-agent information
asymmetry between investment professionals and clients, the
systemic role of trust in capital allocation that turns individual
ethics breaches into market-level externalities, and the
ethical-vs-legal four-quadrant framework that distinguishes
lawful-ethical conduct from lawful-unethical conduct (the gap zone
where the Standards bind even absent legal liability) and from
unlawful conduct (which the Standards never PERMIT regardless of
moral framing). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.255-284.

The Code-of-Ethics material in
[`cc-code-of-ethics.md`](cc-code-of-ethics.md) develops Vol.6
Reading 57's Code text: the six binding principles enumerated
(integrity / competence / diligence; client-and-profession-first;
reasonable care and independent judgment; professional and ethical
manner; promote market integrity; maintain professional
competence), the seven Standards-of-Professional-Conduct headings
(I Professionalism through VII Responsibilities as a CFA Institute
Member or Candidate), the Code-vs-Standards distinction
(constitutional vs. statutory), the Recommended Procedures
distinction (best-practice diligence evidence vs. binding rules),
and the more-strict-of-law-or-Standard rule that anchors
cross-border conduct. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.285-304.

## Chapter 2 Overview

The Standard I (Professionalism) and Standard II (Integrity of
Capital Markets) chapter develops the six sub-standards that govern
the professional's own behavior (I) and the professional's
interaction with capital markets at large (II).

The Standards-I.A-and-I.B material in
[`cc-standard-i-a-b-knowledge-of-law-and-independence.md`](cc-standard-i-a-b-knowledge-of-law-and-independence.md)
develops Standard I.A's knowledge-of-the-law obligation including
the more-strict-of-law-or-Standard cross-border rule and the
affirmative dissociation requirement, plus Standard I.B's
independence-and-objectivity obligation across the three classic
threat families (gift/entertainment from clients-or-brokers,
soft-dollar/client-commission arrangements, issuer-relationship
pressure on analyst ratings). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.305-327.

The Standard-I.C material in
[`cc-standard-i-c-misrepresentation.md`](cc-standard-i-c-misrepresentation.md)
develops the three structurally distinct misrepresentation conduct
classes Vol.6 R58 identifies: plagiarism (verbatim, paraphrased, or
recycled prior-work use without attribution), performance-claim
misstatement (overstated returns, cherry-picked composites, inflated
AUM, hypothetical-as-actual presentation), and material-omission
misrepresentation (true statements rendered misleading by omitted
material context). The knowingly-or-should-have-known predicate
imputes constructive knowledge to professionals who failed to
verify. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.328-339.

The Standard-I.D material in
[`cc-standard-i-d-misconduct.md`](cc-standard-i-d-misconduct.md)
develops the two-prong structure of Vol.6 R58's residual
Professionalism prohibition: the professional-conduct prong
PROHIBITS dishonesty/fraud/deceit in professional activities, and
the residual-reputation prong PROHIBITS any act that reflects
adversely on professional reputation, integrity, or competence. The
private-morality carve-out keeps lawful personal lifestyle choices
outside I.D's reach absent a professional nexus. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.340-342.

The Standard-II.A material in
[`cc-standard-ii-a-material-nonpublic.md`](cc-standard-ii-a-material-nonpublic.md)
develops the two-predicate gate (material AND non-public) that
triggers Vol.6 R58's prohibition on acting on or causing others to
act on material nonpublic information. The mosaic-theory carve-out
PERMITS analyst-derived material conclusions built from non-material
non-public plus public inputs; the disseminate-or-refrain framework
REQUIRES the Member either to cause public dissemination or refrain
from acting; the tipper-tippee chain extends the prohibition to
third parties. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.343-354.

The Standard-II.B material in
[`cc-standard-ii-b-market-manipulation.md`](cc-standard-ii-b-market-manipulation.md)
develops the two-class structure of Vol.6 R58's market-manipulation
prohibition: information-based manipulation (false rumors,
pump-and-dump, bear raids, "talking the book") and
transaction-based manipulation (wash trades, painting the tape,
marking the close, spoofing, layering). The intent-to-mislead
predicate distinguishes manipulation from legitimate market-making,
hedging, liquidity provision, block trading, and arbitrage.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.355-360.

## Chapter 3 Overview

The Standard III (Duties to Clients) and Standard IV (Duties to
Employers) chapter develops the fiduciary surface (III) and the
employer-loyalty surface (IV) that govern the professional's
relationships with the entities conferring the professional role.

The Standards-III.A-and-III.B material in
[`cc-standard-iii-a-b-loyalty-prudence-and-fair-dealing.md`](cc-standard-iii-a-b-loyalty-prudence-and-fair-dealing.md)
develops the fiduciary triad of Vol.6 R58 Standard III.A (loyalty /
prudence / care) and Standard III.B's fair-dealing operationalization
across trade allocation (pro-rata pattern), recommendation
dissemination (wide before trade), IPO allocation (suitability-tier
plus pro-rata within tier), and fee-class differentiation (principled
vs. ad hoc). Cross-link to subcorpus 09's
`pm-investment-policy-statement.md` operationalizes III.A's fiduciary
obligation through the IPS discipline. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.361-378.

The Standards-III.C-D-E material in
[`cc-standard-iii-c-d-e-suitability-performance-confidentiality.md`](cc-standard-iii-c-d-e-suitability-performance-confidentiality.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standard III.C's suitability discipline
(IPS-based for advisory, mandate-relative for fund management),
Standard III.D's fair-accurate-complete performance-presentation
trinity with GIPS as a Recommended Procedure (not a Standard
itself), and Standard III.E's confidentiality default with three
explicit overrides (illegal activity, legal compulsion, client
permission). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.379-395.

The Standard-IV.A material in
[`cc-standard-iv-a-loyalty-to-employer.md`](cc-standard-iv-a-loyalty-to-employer.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standard IV.A's employer-loyalty obligation
including the whistleblower carve-out (PERMITS reporting illegal /
unethical conduct), the post-employment regime distinguishing
skills-and-experience (Member's property) from confidential-files-
and-trade-secrets (employer's property), and the independent-practice
restriction prohibiting competing side business without employer
consent. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.396-405.

The Standards-IV.B-and-IV.C material in
[`cc-standard-iv-b-c-additional-comp-and-responsibilities.md`](cc-standard-iv-b-c-additional-comp-and-responsibilities.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standard IV.B's written-consent gate for
additional compensation that could compete with employer interest,
and Standard IV.C's affirmative detect-prevent-document supervisor
obligation including the inadequate-compliance-system trigger that
captures supervisors who fail to escalate compliance defects. The
IV.B-vs-IV.C distinction (vertical own-compensation duty vs.
horizontal supervision duty) is visually distinguished. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-418.

## Chapter 4 Overview

The Standards V–VII + GIPS Basics + Application Cases chapter
completes the Standards inventory and adds GIPS claim-compliance
basics and one application case study.

The Standard-V material in
[`cc-standard-v-investment-analysis-diligence.md`](cc-standard-v-investment-analysis-diligence.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standards V.A diligence-and-reasonable-basis test
(scaled to investment complexity; third-party-research due-diligence
clause; PROCESS-not-OUTCOME framing), V.B communication-with-clients
discipline (investment-process disclosure, opinion-vs-fact distinction
with hedge-phrase convention, material-change prompt disclosure), and
V.C record-retention obligation (sufficient for ex-post audit of
V.A and V.B). Cross-link to subcorpus 05's
`eq-equity-cost-of-capital-estimation.md` operationalizes V.A for
equity recommendations. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.419-442.

The Standard-VI material in
[`cc-standard-vi-conflicts-of-interest.md`](cc-standard-vi-conflicts-of-interest.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standards VI.A's full-and-fair-disclosure
requirement (prominent placement, plain language, sufficient detail),
VI.B's client-first / employer-second / personal-last priority
hierarchy with the restricted-list and blackout-window Recommended
Procedures, and VI.C's referral-fee disclosure obligation reaching
both client and employer. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.443-460.

The Standard-VII material in
[`cc-standard-vii-responsibilities-as-cfa-institute-member-or-candidate.md`](cc-standard-vii-responsibilities-as-cfa-institute-member-or-candidate.md)
develops Vol.6 R58 Standards VII.A's program-integrity obligations
(exam secrecy, candidate-conduct truthfulness, public-representation
authority) and VII.B's reference rules (exact charter-holder forms,
candidate-status format requiring current enrollment plus next-exam
registration, prohibition on embellishment). **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.461-490.

The GIPS-claim-compliance-basics material in
[`cc-gips-claim-compliance-basics.md`](cc-gips-claim-compliance-basics.md)
develops Vol.6 R59's firm-wide-claim-compliance rule (all
discretionary fee-paying portfolios in composites, partial-firm
claims PROHIBITED), the GIPS-vs-III.D distinction (GIPS is a
Recommended Procedure for III.D compliance but not a Standard
itself), and the explicit boundary with future-15 (attribution
methodology and composite construction depth are out of scope per
DEC-4). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.491-498.

The application-case material in
[`cc-material-info-and-dissemination-delay.md`](cc-material-info-and-dissemination-delay.md)
develops the Ghosh fact-pattern application APPLYING Standard II.A
to a dissemination-delay window between a regulator's investigation
opening and the public announcement: the analyst's
disseminate-or-refrain obligation, the buy-recommendation-withdrawal
mechanism that does not itself signal the non-public information,
and the case-distinguishing reasoning showing why II.A is the
controlling Standard (not I.A, not I.D, not III.A). **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.343-354 (controlling Standard
and case-distinguishing authority).
