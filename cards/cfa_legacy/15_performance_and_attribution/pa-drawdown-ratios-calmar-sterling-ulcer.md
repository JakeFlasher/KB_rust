# Drawdown Ratios: Calmar, Sterling, Burke, Ulcer

## Intuition

Standard deviation punishes upside and downside symmetrically and says nothing
about *path* — how deep a loss got, how long the investor stayed underwater, or
whether several small declines or one catastrophic one drove the volatility. A
whole family of appraisal measures replaces sigma with a *drawdown* statistic to
capture the lived experience of loss: the peak-to-valley fall an investor would
have suffered buying at the high-water mark and selling at the low. Each ratio in
the family is the same Sharpe-type reward-per-unit-risk object — excess return
over a risk measure — differing only in *which* facet of drawdown sits in the
denominator: the single worst loss (Calmar), an average of the largest losses
(Sterling), a root-sum-of-squares that penalises a few big losses over many mild
ones (Burke), or a time-and-depth blend (Martin/Pain). As Bacon warns, most are
"fairly redundant with the notable exception of maximum drawdown."

**Source:** Bacon (2022) §5 (Drawdown) pp.129-133

## Definition

The denominators are built from the return path's underwater geometry:

- **Maximum drawdown** `DMax` — the single maximum peak-to-valley loss over the
  window (mini-max), "not to be confused with the largest individual drawdown."
- **Largest individual drawdown** `DLar` — the largest *uninterrupted* peak-to-
  valley loss; the Sterling denominator averages a fixed count `d` of these.
- **Drawdown deviation** `DD` — a "standard deviation type statistic using
  individual drawdowns," the root-mean-square of the `d` individual drawdowns.
- **Ulcer index** `UI` — root-mean-square of the drawdown-from-previous-peak in
  *every* period (RMS underwater), combining depth with time under water.
- **Pain index** `PI` — the *mean* of the absolute drawdown-from-peak across all
  periods (un-squared Ulcer): mean underwater depth.

The ratios are excess return `r - rF` (or geometric excess `g`) over one of these:

> Calmar `CR = (r - rF) / DMax`   (MAR = same, but from inception not 36m)
> Sterling `SRd = (r - rF) / DLar`   (DLar = mean of the `d` largest)
> Burke `BRd = (r - rF) / sqrt(sum Dj^2)`; Modified Burke uses `DD`
> Martin `MR = (r - rF) / UI`   (Ulcer Performance Index)
> Pain ratio `PR = (r - rF) / PI`

**Source:** Bacon (2022) §5 (Drawdown) eq.5.2-5.12 pp.131-136

## Mathematical Reasoning

The family is generated by a single substitution: hold the numerator fixed at the
excess return and swap the denominator risk functional. Writing `D'i` for the
drawdown-from-previous-peak in period `i` over `n` periods, the depth/duration
denominators are nested norms of the underwater series `{D'i}`:

> Pain index `PI = (1/n) sum |D'i|`           (an L1 / mean norm)
> Ulcer index `UI = sqrt((1/n) sum D'i^2)`     (an L2 / RMS norm)

Because squaring then averaging then square-rooting (the L2 norm) weights large
entries more heavily than the simple mean (the L1 norm), `UI >= PI` always (a
consequence of the power-mean inequality on the non-negative `|D'i|`), so the
Martin ratio is no larger than the Pain ratio for the same numerator. The same
ordering logic explains Burke's design: Bacon notes Burke uses "the square root of
the sum of the squares of each drawdown in order to penalise major drawdowns as
opposed to many mild ones" — the L2 aggregation over individual drawdowns `Dj`
makes one deep loss cost more than several shallow ones of equal total depth.

Two denominators coincide structurally. The Modified Burke denominator
`sqrt(sum Dj^2/n)` is exactly the drawdown deviation `DD`, and since dividing every
ranked portfolio's denominator by the constant `sqrt(n)` cannot reorder them,
Bacon states "both the modified and standard Burke ratios will generate identical
portfolio rankings." Likewise Sterling collapses to Calmar in the limit: fixing
the averaging count `d = 1` makes `DLar` the single largest individual drawdown,
and the Sterling-Calmar variant simply substitutes the *average annual* maximum
drawdown for `DMax`.

A monotonicity caveat: Bacon, "Unlike negative Sharpe and negative information
ratios," sees no "great deal of value in negative" Calmar ratios, and extends the
same skepticism to the active versions ("I see no value in negative active Pain,
Ulcer and Calmar ratios") — dividing a negative excess return by a positive
drawdown does not preserve the usual reward-per-risk interpretation, so the family
is informative only when `r - rF > 0`.

**Source:** Bacon (2022) §5 (Drawdown / Active drawdown) eq.5.3-5.11 pp.131-136, 140

## Boundary Notes

These ratios measure *absolute* drawdown by default. Computed on relative/active
return (underperformance vs benchmark) they become the Active Pain, Active Ulcer,
and Active Calmar ratios, with the numerator switched to geometric excess return
`g` and the denominator built from active drawdowns `AD'j`. Active versions belong
with tracking-error / active-risk treatment, not absolute risk-adjusted return.

**Source:** Bacon (2022) §5 (Active drawdown) eq.5.13-5.15 pp.139-140
