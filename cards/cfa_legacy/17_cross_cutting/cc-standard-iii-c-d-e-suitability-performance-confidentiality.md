# Standard III(C) Suitability + III(D) Performance Presentation + III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality

## Intuition

Standard III.C REQUIRES Members and Candidates to make a reasonable
inquiry into a client's investment experience, risk and return
objectives, and financial constraints before making any investment
recommendation or taking investment action, AND to ensure
recommended investments are consistent with the client's mandate.
The suitability analysis ties directly to the IPS for advisory
clients and to the fund mandate for fund management. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.379-386.

Standard III.D REQUIRES Members and Candidates to make reasonable
efforts to ensure that investment performance information
communicated to clients is fair, accurate, and complete. The
Standard's Recommended Procedure (RECOMMENDS) is to apply GIPS;
GIPS itself is voluntary, not a Standard. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.387-391.

Standard III.E REQUIRES Members and Candidates to keep information
about current, former, and prospective clients confidential UNLESS
the information concerns illegal activities, disclosure is required
by law, or the client permits disclosure. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.392-395.

## Definition

Standard III.C REQUIRES Members and Candidates, in advisory
relationships, to: (1) make a reasonable inquiry into a client's
or prospective client's investment experience, risk and return
objectives, and financial constraints prior to making any
investment recommendation or taking investment action and update
the inquiry at material life events; (2) determine that an
investment is suitable to the client's financial situation and is
consistent with the client's written objectives, mandates, and
constraints before making a recommendation or taking action; (3)
judge the suitability of investments in the context of the
client's total portfolio. For fund management, the suitability
analysis is mandate-relative: recommendations must align with the
fund's stated investment strategy, style, and constraints. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.379-386.

Standard III.D REQUIRES Members and Candidates to make reasonable
efforts to ensure that performance information is fair (composite
selection does not cherry-pick winners), accurate (figures are
arithmetically correct), and complete (context — fee class, time
period, benchmark — is disclosed). The Recommended Procedures
identify GIPS as the canonical performance-presentation standard
but DO NOT REQUIRE GIPS compliance; a firm can satisfy III.D
without claiming GIPS compliance, and a firm can claim GIPS
compliance while still violating III.D if the presentation omits
material context. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.387-391.

Standard III.E REQUIRES Members and Candidates to keep client
information confidential UNLESS one of three overrides applies: the
information concerns illegal activities (REQUIRES disclosure to
appropriate authorities under separate Standards or law); disclosure
is required by law (e.g., subpoena, regulatory examination); the
client expressly permits disclosure. The duty extends to prospective
clients (the duty attaches when the inquiry begins, not when the
account is opened) and to former clients (the duty survives the
end of the advisory relationship). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022)
L1 Vol.6/pp.392-395.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The IPS-based suitability framework under III.C (source REQUIRES)
ties III.C to the existing 09 IPS card: the IPS is the written
record of the client's mandate; III.C REQUIRES every recommendation
to be consistent with the IPS. The Recommended Procedure RECOMMENDS
the IPS be updated annually and at material life events (job
change, marriage / divorce, inheritance, retirement, major medical);
recommendations made under a stale IPS may violate III.C even when
the recommendation appears reasonable in absolute terms. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.379-384.

The fair-accurate-complete trinity under III.D (source REQUIRES)
maps to three distinct violation predicates: (1) unfair composite
selection (e.g., showing only the top-quartile accounts in a
multi-account composite); (2) inaccurate figures (e.g., gross-of-fee
returns presented as net-of-fee); (3) incomplete context (e.g.,
hypothetical returns presented without disclosure that they are
hypothetical). A single performance presentation can violate any
combination of the three predicates; the Standard requires the
presentation to satisfy ALL three simultaneously. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.387-391.

The III.E confidentiality default (source REQUIRES) sets a
client-information shield with three explicit carve-outs. The
illegal-activities override (REQUIRES disclosure to appropriate
authorities under separate Standards) and the legal-compulsion
override (REQUIRES compliance with subpoena/regulatory inquiry)
are both binding; the client-permission override (PERMITS
disclosure when the client expressly permits it) is the only
override that turns on client autonomy. The Recommended Procedures
suggest erring on the side of confidentiality when an override is
ambiguous; the Standard's text does NOT permit silent disclosure
even when the Member believes disclosure would benefit the client.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.392-395.
