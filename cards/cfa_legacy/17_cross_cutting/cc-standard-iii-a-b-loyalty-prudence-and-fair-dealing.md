# Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care + III(B) Fair Dealing

## Intuition

Standard III.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates to act for the
benefit of their clients with the care, skill, and diligence that a
prudent professional would exercise, and to place client interests
above their employer's and their own. III.A is the fiduciary
anchor of the Standards: the duty operationalizes the trust
framework by REQUIRING client-first conduct in every concrete
decision (trade allocation, security selection, voting, soft-dollar
use, fee disclosure). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.361-369.

Standard III.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to deal fairly and
objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis,
making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, or
engaging in other professional activities. "Fairly" does NOT mean
"equally": different fee classes and different account mandates
appropriately receive different treatment. Fairness instead REQUIRES
that the differential treatment be principled (consistent rules,
disclosed in advance) rather than ad hoc (favorable treatment for
one client based on relationship strength rather than mandate or
fee class). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.370-378.

## Definition

Standard III.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates to: (1) act for the
benefit of their clients and place their clients' interests before
their own and their employer's; (2) use reasonable care and exercise
prudent judgment in client investment activities; (3) act with
loyalty to clients. The Recommended Procedures include submitting
to a written firm policy that defines the prudent-professional
standard, documenting the basis for investment decisions, voting
proxies in the client's interest, applying soft-dollar arrangements
only to research that benefits the client, and disclosing all
relevant compensation arrangements. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.361-369.

Standard III.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to deal fairly and
objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis,
making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, or
engaging in other professional activities. The Recommended
Procedures include having a fair, equitable trade-allocation
procedure (pro-rata across participating accounts, with documented
deviations); disseminating recommendations widely before trading on
them; allocating IPOs by client suitability and pro-rata within
suitability tiers; and disclosing fee differentials so clients
understand how their account compares to other accounts.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.370-378.

The fiduciary duty under III.A is mandate-relative: the prudent
professional standard depends on the client's mandate (an aggressive
growth mandate permits higher-volatility positions than a
preservation-of-capital mandate); the prudence test asks what a
prudent professional managing the SAME mandate would do, not what
a prudent professional managing a generic balanced fund would do.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.361-369.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The fiduciary triad of III.A (source REQUIRES) operationalizes the
trust foundation into three concrete obligations whose violation
the PCP can adjudicate: loyalty (place client first), prudence
(exercise professional care), care (give attention proportional to
mandate complexity). The Standards' Guidance ties III.A directly to
the Investment Policy Statement (IPS) discipline of subcorpus 09:
the IPS defines the client's mandate; III.A REQUIRES the manager
to honor that mandate in every concrete decision. The
Repo-touchpoints link to `pm-investment-policy-statement.md` is
the cross-vertical anchor. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.361-369.

The fair-dealing operationalization (source REQUIRES) draws the
line between principled differentiation (allowed) and ad hoc
favoritism (prohibited): a fee-class structure that gives lower
fees to larger accounts is principled because the differentiation
is documented in advance and tied to a quantifiable feature (size);
a "best-clients-only" early-recommendation disclosure is ad hoc
because the differentiation is relationship-based and not tied to
mandate. The principled-vs-ad hoc distinction is the most
exam-tested fair-dealing predicate; the Recommended Procedures
suggest documenting the differentiation rule in writing and
applying it consistently. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.370-378.

The trade-allocation Recommended Procedures (source RECOMMENDS) for
III.B specify the pro-rata-across-participating-accounts default:
when multiple accounts are simultaneously interested in a security,
the trade is allocated pro-rata across the participating accounts
(by AUM, position size, or other documented metric), with any
deviation from pro-rata documented and justified (e.g., a small
account fully filled because pro-rata would have given it a
non-economic share). The Recommended Procedures do not REQUIRE
pro-rata as a Standard; the violation predicate is unfair-
allocation, which pro-rata satisfies but other documented rules
can also satisfy. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.373-378.
