# Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law and I(B) Independence and Objectivity

## Intuition

Standard I.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates to understand and
comply with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations of any
government, regulatory organization, licensing agency, or
professional association governing their professional activities,
AND to comply with the more strict of the Code/Standards or
applicable law. The "more strict" rule is the cross-border anchor:
a charter-holder in a permissive jurisdiction must still meet the
Standards. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.305-314.

Standard I.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to use reasonable care
and judgment to achieve and maintain independence and objectivity
in their professional activities. The most-tested practical
applications are gifts and entertainment (modest token gifts from
clients are PERMITTED with employer disclosure; lavish gifts from
brokers/issuers compromise objectivity), soft-dollar arrangements
(client-commission-funded research is PERMITTED only when client
interests are paramount), and analyst-coverage pressure (an issuer
threatening to revoke management access if the analyst rates
"sell" is exactly the independence threat I.B prohibits the analyst
from bowing to). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.315-327.

## Definition

Standard I.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates to: (1) understand
and comply with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations,
including the Code and Standards, of any government, regulatory
organization, licensing agency, or professional association
governing their professional activities; (2) not knowingly
participate or assist in and dissociate from any violation of
such laws, rules, or regulations; (3) comply with the more strict
of the Code/Standards or applicable law when the two differ.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.305-310.

Standard I.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to: (1) use reasonable
care and judgment to achieve and maintain independence and
objectivity in their professional activities; (2) not offer,
solicit, or accept any gift, benefit, compensation, or
consideration that reasonably could be expected to compromise
their own or another's independence and objectivity. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.315-320.

The Recommended Procedures for I.A (RECOMMENDS) include staying
informed via continuing education, reviewing employer compliance
policies, and seeking advice of counsel when applicable law is
ambiguous. For I.B (RECOMMENDS), they include written
firm-policies-on-gifts, formal independence reviews for analysts,
restrictions on personal relationships with covered issuers, and
careful soft-dollar policy administration with client-benefit
documentation. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.310-314,
320-327.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The more-strict-of-law-or-Standard rule (source REQUIRES) creates a
two-dimensional conduct space indexed by (local law treatment,
Standards treatment) for any conduct X. The binding obligation is
the strictest treatment across the two dimensions. When local law
permits X but Standards prohibit X, the Member must NOT engage in X.
When local law prohibits X but Standards permit X, the Member must
NOT engage in X. The intuition is that charter-holders accept a
floor of conduct (the Standards) that travels with them across
jurisdictions, supplemented by whatever additional strictness the
local jurisdiction adds. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.307-310.

The independence threat taxonomy under I.B (source ASSERTS) catalogs
three structural threats: (1) gift/entertainment threats from
clients, brokers, or issuers, addressable by employer-policy
disclosure and modesty norms; (2) soft-dollar threats arising when
client-funded commissions purchase research or services, addressable
by ensuring the research benefits the client (not the firm); (3)
issuer-pressure threats arising when management restricts access in
retaliation for unfavorable ratings, addressable by maintaining the
rating despite the pressure (the independence threat is the test,
not the financial harm). The taxonomy REQUIRES affirmative action
to maintain independence; passive avoidance is insufficient when
the conduct compromises objectivity. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.315-322.

The dissociation requirement under I.A (source REQUIRES) governs
the case where the Member observes a colleague's or firm's
violation of applicable law: the Member must NOT knowingly
participate or assist in the violation, and SHOULD dissociate from
the activity (e.g., refuse to sign off, leave the meeting, decline
the assignment). Failure to dissociate makes the Member complicit
even if the Member did not initiate the violation. The Recommended
Procedure further suggests reporting to compliance or, in extreme
cases, public authority — though the latter is not a Standard
requirement, only a recommendation. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.308-313.

Open CFA L1 Vol.6 Reading 58 directly (Standard I.A section pp.305-314
and Standard I.B section pp.315-327) for the full application examples
and the analyst-pressure case studies that the card omits for length.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.305-327.

- The reader needs the full Recommended Procedures detail for I.A or
  I.B; the card lists the high-level recommendations but omits the
  enumerated firm-policy templates. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022)
  L1 Vol.6/pp.310-314, 320-327.
- The reader needs the analyst-pressure application examples (the
  classic "issuer threatens to revoke management access" scenarios)
  that exam questions draw from; the card states the prohibition
  abstractly. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.322-327.
- The reader needs cross-jurisdictional regulatory specifics (SEC
  rule 17a-3 record retention; UK FCA disclosure rules; MiFID II
  research-cost separation) that interact with I.A's "more strict"
  rule. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.307-314.
