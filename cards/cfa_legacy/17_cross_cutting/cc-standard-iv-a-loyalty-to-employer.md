# Standard IV(A) Loyalty (to Employer)

## Intuition

Standard IV.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates, in matters related to
their employment, to act for the benefit of their employer AND not
deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and
abilities, divulge confidential information, OR otherwise cause harm
to the employer. The Standard frames the Member-employer relationship
as a duty of loyalty subject to two carve-outs: (1) the
whistleblower carve-out PERMITS reporting illegal/unethical conduct
even when it disadvantages the employer; (2) the independent-judgment
carve-out PERMITS the Member to follow their own ethical compass
when the employer asks them to violate the Standards. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.396-405.

The post-employment regime distinguishes "skills and experience"
(which the Member is free to take) from "client lists, contact
files, employer property" (which the Member must leave with the
employer). A departing Member may compete for business and use
publicly available client contact information; the Member may NOT
take confidential client files, proprietary research, or trade
secrets. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.401-405.

## Definition

Standard IV.A REQUIRES Members and Candidates, in matters related to
their employment, to: (1) act for the benefit of their employer and
not deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and
abilities; (2) keep employer information confidential; (3) refrain
from engaging in independent practice in competition with the
employer without employer consent. The duty extends to all
employment-related matters and survives employment for confidential
information and trade secrets. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.396-401.

The whistleblower carve-out (PERMITS) reaches conduct that would
otherwise violate IV.A's loyalty duty: a Member who reports the
employer's illegal or unethical conduct to compliance, regulators,
or in some cases the public has NOT violated IV.A because IV.A's
duty is to the employer's lawful interests; reporting unlawful
conduct serves the employer's lawful interest in compliance even
when it disadvantages the employer's short-term position. The
carve-out is narrow: the conduct must be illegal or unethical (mere
disagreement with management is not enough); the report should
follow the firm's internal compliance escalation before going
external. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.398-401.

The independent-practice restriction (REQUIRES) prohibits competing
side business without employer consent: a Member may not start a
competing investment advisory practice or take on competing clients
on the side without express employer permission. The Standard does
NOT prohibit non-competing side activities (e.g., teaching,
writing, board service) provided those activities do not interfere
with employer duties and do not violate other Standards (e.g.,
VI.A disclosure of additional compensation). **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.401-404.

The post-employment client-solicitation rules (PERMITS) allow a
departing Member to solicit former clients using publicly available
contact information; the Member may NOT use confidential client
information taken from the former employer's files. The line is
between memory and documents: the Member's memory of clients is
permissible to use; physical or digital copies of client lists are
employer property and must be left behind. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.404-405.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The duty-of-loyalty structure (source REQUIRES) operationalizes the
Code's principle 2 (place integrity of profession and client
interest above personal interest) by extending the agent-principal
loyalty model to the Member-employer relationship. The Member is
the agent; the employer is the principal; the Standard REQUIRES
the agent to act for the principal's benefit and not for the
agent's own competing interest. The agent-principal model also
explains the whistleblower carve-out: a principal engaged in
illegal conduct has no legitimate interest in the agent
participating in or concealing the conduct, so the agent's
disclosure does not breach the loyalty duty. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.396-401.

The post-employment regime (source PERMITS/REQUIRES) draws a
defensible line: skills and experience are the Member's property
(the employer paid for training but the result is internalized in
the Member); confidential client information and trade secrets are
the employer's property (the employer paid for client acquisition
and product development). The Standards' Guidance specifies that
even a Member who manages to memorize a complete client list may
not use that memory in a way that takes economic advantage of the
employer's investment in client relationships; the memorization
mechanism does not launder the confidential information. The
Recommended Procedures suggest signing a separate confidentiality
agreement on departure that documents the line. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.401-405.

The whistleblower carve-out's narrow scope (source PERMITS) is
the most exam-tested IV.A predicate: the carve-out PERMITS
disclosure ONLY when the conduct is illegal or unethical; mere
disagreement with management strategy, dissatisfaction with
compensation, or personality conflict does NOT trigger the
whistleblower exception. The Recommended Procedures further
suggest exhausting internal compliance escalation before going
external (compliance officer → senior compliance → general counsel
→ regulator → public), though the Standard does not REQUIRE that
sequence in all cases — for ongoing fraud or serious investor harm,
external disclosure may be appropriate immediately. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.398-401.
