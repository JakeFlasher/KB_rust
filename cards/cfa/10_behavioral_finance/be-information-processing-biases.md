# Information-Processing Biases

## Intuition

Where belief-perseverance biases concern *defending* a belief, information-processing biases concern the upstream mechanics of *perceiving, coding, and recalling* the information from which beliefs are built. The distortion enters before the belief is even settled: an arbitrary number anchors an estimate, money is sorted into nonfungible mental buckets, a problem's framing flips risk attitude, a vivid memory inflates a probability, success is credited to self and failure to luck, and recent observations crowd out older ones. Anchoring and adjustment is the prototype — a psychological heuristic that influences the way people intuit probabilities, where investors cling to arbitrary "purchase points" or price levels and adjust insufficiently away from them.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.6 pp.76.

The other members each corrupt a different stage of processing. Mental accounting codes, categorizes, and evaluates economic outcomes by grouping assets into nonfungible mental accounts, so a dollar is treated differently by source or label. Framing changes preferences purely through how a problem is presented (the gain/loss medicine framing, narrow framing). Availability estimates probabilities by ease of recall rather than by statistics. Self-attribution credits successes to skill and blames failures on bad luck, feeding overconfidence. Recency over-weights the most recently observed data — the source of performance-chasing into last year's hot asset class.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.16 pp.171, Ch.22 pp.239, Ch.8 pp.94, Ch.9 pp.104, Ch.20 pp.216.

## Definition

**Information-processing biases** (2e umbrella) are the cognitive biases arising from the mechanics of handling information: anchoring & adjustment, mental accounting, framing, availability, self-attribution, and recency.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Pt.Two pp.49-50.

**Anchoring and adjustment** is a heuristic by which people begin estimation from an initial default number (the anchor), then adjust insufficiently, producing biased final estimates that cling to statistically arbitrary, psychologically determined reference points such as purchase prices.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.6 pp.75-76.

**Mental accounting** is the tendency to code, categorize, and evaluate economic outcomes by grouping assets into nonfungible mental accounts, treating sums differently by their source (work, inheritance, gambling, bonus) or intended use.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.16 pp.171.

**Framing bias** is the tendency to respond differently to the same situation based on the context in which a choice is presented; framing effects occur when preferences change as a function of some variation in framing, including *narrow framing* (focusing too restrictively on one or two aspects).
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.22 pp.237-239.

**Availability bias** is the rule of thumb that estimates an outcome's probability by how easily examples come to mind; Pompian's four investor-relevant sources are retrievability, categorization, narrow range of experience, and resonance.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.8 pp.94-96.

**Self-attribution bias** is the tendency to ascribe successes to innate dispositional factors (talent, foresight) and failures to situational factors (bad luck), splitting into self-enhancing and self-protecting components.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.9 pp.104-105.

**Recency bias** is the cognitive predisposition to recall and emphasize recent events more prominently than near or distant past ones; technically the *recency effect* on the U-shaped serial-position curve, recall from short-term memory.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.20 pp.216-218.

## Mathematical Reasoning

Framing is the family's cleanest formal violation of rational choice: it breaks *description invariance*. The same prospect framed as a gain ("25 percent of patients will be saved") versus a loss ("75 percent of patients will die") flips most people from a risk-averse choice to a risk-seeking one, even though the two descriptions are extensionally identical. Writing the decision as `choice = f(frame, prospect)` rather than `choice = f(prospect)` captures the defect: a rational agent's preference should depend on the prospect only, so `f(frame_1, X) = f(frame_2, X)` whenever frame_1 and frame_2 describe the same X. (The source asserts the empirical flip and defines the frame without a parametric model.)
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.22 pp.239.

Mental accounting violates *fungibility*: a rational agent values wealth by total `W`, but a mental accounter partitions `W = sum_k A_k` into nonfungible accounts `A_k` and applies account-specific valuations, so the marginal propensity to consume varies with the source label of a dollar even when the dollar is identical (Thaler's $30 windfall experiment: 70% gamble when the $30 is "found" money but only 34% when the same payoffs are framed as terminal outcomes). Recency, by contrast, is a recall-weighting distortion: on the serial-position curve, recall probability spikes for the most recent items (short-term memory) so recent observations receive excess weight in frequency and probability estimates.
**Source:** Pompian (2006) Ch.16 pp.172, Ch.20 pp.217-218.
