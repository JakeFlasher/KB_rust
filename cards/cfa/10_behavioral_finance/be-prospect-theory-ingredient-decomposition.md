# Prospect-Theory Ingredient Decomposition of Anomaly Spreads

## Intuition

Having shown the model helps explain 14 anomalies, BJW ask which of prospect theory's three risk-attitude ingredients -- loss aversion, diminishing sensitivity, or probability weighting -- actually does the work. They decompose each anomaly's model-predicted alpha spread into the part generated by loss aversion alone, the marginal part added by probability weighting, and the marginal part added by diminishing sensitivity, then average the fractions across the 14 explained anomalies.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.43.

The average fractions are `-21%` for loss aversion, `48%` for probability weighting, and `73%` for diminishing sensitivity. The negative fraction for loss aversion is telling: taken alone, loss aversion predicts an alpha spread of the *opposite* sign from the data, because higher volatility raises the required return on the low-alpha decile -- the wrong direction. Diminishing sensitivity appears to dominate, but this understates probability weighting's role.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.44.

The deeper point is that probability weighting is what *enables* diminishing sensitivity to have a large effect. With only loss aversion and diminishing sensitivity the model explains just 8 of 23 anomalies; adding probability weighting raises that to 14 -- almost doubling the count. Because the typical stock in any decile is positively skewed, probability weighting lowers the expected return on all deciles; for a stock at a prior loss, many of its potential gains and losses then stay in the convex (risk-seeking) region of the value function, lowering its expected return further. So the two ingredients act jointly, and the headline `73%` for diminishing sensitivity cannot be read in isolation from the `48%` for probability weighting.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.44.

## Definition

**Loss aversion (lambda)** -- the kink at the origin making losses loom larger than gains; in this model it links higher return volatility to a *higher* required return, the force that works against the anomaly spreads.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.43-44.

**Probability weighting (delta)** -- overweighting of distributional tails; for positively skewed stocks it lowers required returns and, crucially, is the ingredient that lets diminishing sensitivity exert a large effect, nearly doubling the anomalies explained.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.44.

**Diminishing sensitivity (alpha)** -- concavity over gains and convexity over losses; via the prior-gain mechanism (a prior gain brings investors into the concave risk-averse region) it generates the largest gross fraction of the explained spreads.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.44.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The decomposition turns ingredients on cumulatively by adjusting the preference parameters. Let `alpha_LA(j)` be the model alpha for decile `j` under loss aversion only, `(alpha, delta, lambda) = (1, 1, 1.5)`, and `alpha_LA,PW(j)` the alpha under loss aversion plus probability weighting, `(1, 0.65, 1.5)`. The full-model spread decomposes as

```
  alpha_m(10) - alpha_m(1)
    = [ alpha_LA(10)    - alpha_LA(1) ]                          (loss aversion)
    + [ (alpha_LA,PW(10) - alpha_LA,PW(1)) - (alpha_LA(10) - alpha_LA(1)) ]   (prob. weighting)
    + [ (alpha_m(10)    - alpha_m(1))   - (alpha_LA,PW(10) - alpha_LA,PW(1)) ] (dim. sensitivity)
```

The three bracketed rows are the contributions of loss aversion, the marginal contribution of probability weighting, and the marginal contribution of diminishing sensitivity, respectively. Averaging the fraction each contributes across the 14 anomalies gives `-21%`, `48%`, `73%`.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.43-44.

A complementary count confirms the joint role: the loss-aversion-plus-diminishing-sensitivity specification `(alpha, delta, lambda)` with `delta = 1` (no weighting) explains only 8 anomalies, while removing the prior gain entirely (`g_i = 0`, so no narrow-framing/integrated-gain channel) collapses the count to just 5 of 23. The narrow-framing and integrated-prior-gain assumptions are therefore load-bearing, not cosmetic.
**Source:** Barberis, Jin & Wang (2021) §V.A pp.44-45.

```
  Ingredient                avg. fraction of spread   note
  ----------------------    -----------------------   ------------------------
  loss aversion              -21%                     wrong sign on its own
  probability weighting       48%                     enables dim. sensitivity
  diminishing sensitivity     73%                     largest gross share
```
