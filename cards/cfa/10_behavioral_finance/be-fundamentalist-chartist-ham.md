# Fundamentalist vs Chartist Heterogeneous-Agent Model

## Intuition

The heterogeneous-agent model (HAM) replaces the single rational representative agent with a small set of behavioral "types" whose forecasting rules clash. The canonical two-type setup pits **fundamentalists**, who expect prices to revert toward a fundamental value, against **chartists** (trend followers), who extrapolate recent price movements. Because chartist demand pushes the price in the direction it has already moved, it sustains and reinforces trends and amplifies deviations from fundamental value; fundamentalist demand pulls back toward the fundamental. The tension between these two expectational forces is what generates rich endogenous dynamics — booms, busts, and mean reversion — without any exogenous news shock. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2 pp.260-265.

The mechanism matters because it offers a structural micro-foundation for the price patterns behavioral finance documents only as anomalies. The model's "expectation feedback" loop is self-referential: agents forecast endogenous prices, and those forecasts, transmitted through a market maker, determine the actual prices realized. This co-evolution of beliefs and prices is the engine of the model, and it links directly to the sentiment-vs-fundamentals split and the two-model mispricing taxonomy of behavioral asset pricing. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §1 pp.259-259.

The setup is deliberately parsimonious — few belief-types, reduced parameters — which keeps it analytically tractable within the framework of nonlinear dynamical systems while still reproducing stylized facts. It is the discrete-time backbone on which the Brock-Hommes switching dynamics and the rational-route-to-randomness bifurcation results are built. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §1 pp.260-260.

## Definition

**Fundamentalist (fundamental trader)** is an agent who places orders in view of a mean reversion of the asset price toward its fundamental in the long run; its conditional mean forecast is `E_{1,t}(p_{t+1}) = p_t + (1 - alpha)[E_t(p*_{t+1}) - p_t]`, where `1 - alpha` is the speed of expected adjustment toward the fundamental `p*`. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.265-265.

**Chartist (trend follower)** is an agent who relies on extrapolative rules; its forecast `E_{2,t}(p_{t+1}) = p_t + gamma(p_t - u_t)` adds an extrapolation of the trend, with `gamma >= 0` measuring the strength of extrapolation and `u_t` a (geometrically decaying) sample mean of past prices. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.266-266.

**Market maker** is the agent who absorbs the net order flow and adjusts price linearly: `p_{t+1} = p_t + lambda Z_{e,t} + delta_t`, where `Z_{e,t}` is the population-weighted average order flow, `lambda` the speed of price adjustment, and `delta_t` a noise-trader disturbance. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.265-265.

## Mathematical Reasoning

Consider one risky and one risk-free asset, constant rate `r`, price `p_t`, dividend `d_t`. Excess return is `R_{t+1} = p_{t+1} + d_{t+1} - R p_t` with `R = 1 + r`. The order flow of type-`h` investors is the myopic mean-variance demand `z_{h,t} = E_{h,t}(R_{t+1}) / (a_h V_{h,t}(R_{t+1}))`, where `a_h` is risk aversion. With population fractions `Q_{1,t}, Q_{2,t}` for fundamentalists and trend followers and `Q_{3,t} = n_3` for noise traders, the average order flow is `Z_{e,t} = Q_{1,t} z_{1,t} + Q_{2,t} z_{2,t} + n_3 xi_t`. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.265-265.

The fundamental value follows a random walk `p*_{t+1} = p*_t exp(-sigma_eps^2/2 + sigma_eps eps_{t+1})`, so `E_t(p*_{t+1}) = p*_t`. The parameter `alpha in (0,1)` measures confidence in convergence: a high `alpha` means slower adjustment of the market price to the fundamental. The trend followers' variance `V_{2,t}(p_{t+1}) = sigma_1^2 + b_2 v_t` is sensitive to a sample variance `v_t`, and both `u_t` and `v_t` are updated by geometric decay with rate `delta in (0,1)`. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.266-266.

```
  expectation-feedback loop (two-type HAM)
                +----------------------+
                |  fundamentalists     |  E_{1,t}: revert to p*
   beliefs ---> |    vs                | --> orders z_{1,t}, z_{2,t}
                |  chartists           |  E_{2,t}: extrapolate trend
                +----------+-----------+
                           |  average order flow Z_{e,t}
                           v
                  market maker: p_{t+1} = p_t + lambda Z_{e,t} + delta_t
                           |
                           +----> realized price feeds next-period beliefs
```

Substituting demands into the price-adjustment rule yields a low-dimensional nonlinear dynamic system in `(p_t, u_t, v_t)` (plus the type fractions). The deterministic skeleton has a unique fundamental steady state at `p = p*`, around which the interplay of mean reversion and extrapolation governs local stability and the shape of fluctuations. **Source:** Dieci and He (2018) §2.1 pp.266-266.
