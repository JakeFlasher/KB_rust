# Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct

## Intuition

The Code of Ethics consists of six normative principles that
Members and Candidates MUST adopt; the Standards of Professional
Conduct are seven binding rule headings (I through VII) that
elaborate the Code into specific testable obligations. The Code
states the WHY (act with integrity, place client first, etc.); the
Standards state the WHAT and HOW (the specific conduct
requirements). **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.285-295.

The structure mirrors law: the Code is constitutional (broad,
principle-level); the Standards are statutory (specific, enforceable
rules); Vol.6 Reading 58 Guidance is regulatory commentary; Vol.6
"Recommended Procedures" subsections are best-practice industry
guides. A violation is judged primarily against Standards text
(statutory), with Code principles framing the spirit. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.295-304.

## Definition

The Code of Ethics REQUIRES Members and Candidates to: (1) act with
integrity, competence, diligence, respect, and an ethical manner
with the public, clients, prospective clients, employers, employees,
colleagues, and other participants in capital markets; (2) place the
integrity of the investment profession and the interests of clients
above their own personal interests; (3) use reasonable care and
exercise independent professional judgment when conducting
investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking
investment actions, and engaging in other professional activities;
(4) practice and encourage others to practice in a professional and
ethical manner that will reflect credit on themselves and the
profession; (5) promote the integrity and viability of the global
capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society; (6) maintain
and improve their professional competence and strive to maintain
and improve the competence of other investment professionals.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.287-291.

The Standards of Professional Conduct REQUIRE conduct elaborated
across seven headings (each with two to five sub-standards). The
seven headings are the binding rule families that drive the per-
sub-standard cards in v8's Batches 1-2. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.291-296.

Members and Candidates voluntarily adopt the Code and Standards by
joining CFA Institute and by registering for the CFA Program; the
adoption is enforced by the Professional Conduct Program (PCP), a
disciplinary mechanism that can issue private censure, public
censure, suspension of membership / charter / candidacy, or
revocation of the CFA designation. Disciplinary decisions are
governed by procedural fairness rules outside this card's scope.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.296-304.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The Code-Standards distinction (source ASSERTS, not proves) maps
to a two-layer obligation structure: Code principles are framed as
broad duties whose violation requires a Standards-text predicate to
be actionable; Standards I-VII are framed as specific predicates
whose violation is directly actionable by the PCP. The Code's
preamble obligation REQUIRES adoption of the Code as a whole; the
Standards REQUIRE specific conduct (e.g., II.A REQUIRES not acting
on material nonpublic information; III.D REQUIRES fair, accurate,
and complete performance presentation). A complaint to the PCP
identifies the violated Standard; the Code provides interpretive
context. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.291-296.

The Standards-vs-Recommended-Procedures distinction (source
ASSERTS) is the most common source of conduct misinterpretation:
each Standards Roman-numeral heading in Vol.6 R58 has THREE
subsections — the Standard text (REQUIRES / PROHIBITS), the
Guidance commentary (interprets the Standard's scope), and the
Recommended Procedures (RECOMMENDS best-practice implementation).
A Member or Candidate who failed to follow a Recommended Procedure
has NOT necessarily violated the Standard; the Standard's text is
the violation predicate. Recommended Procedures are diligence
evidence in a PCP investigation but are not free-standing
obligations. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.291-296.

The "more strict of law or Standard" rule (source REQUIRES) governs
cross-border conduct: a Member or Candidate operating in a
jurisdiction where local law is less strict than the Standards must
follow the Standards; where local law is more strict, the local law
applies. This rule is elaborated in Standard I.A and reframes the
ethical-vs-legal quadrant from the trust card: even when local law
PERMITS a conduct, the Standards REQUIRE the stricter behavior of
charter-holders. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.293-296.

## Escalate to Raw When

Open CFA L1 Vol.6 Reading 57 directly for the full Code text, the
Standards-of-Practice Handbook history, the Professional Conduct
Program procedural rules, and the disciplinary-decision precedent
that the card omits. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
Vol.6/pp.285-304.

- The reader needs the full Code text (six principles verbatim) for
  exam-style memorization; the card paraphrases. **Source:** CFA
  Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.287-291.
- The reader needs the Standards-of-Practice Handbook's historical
  evolution (the Code dates to 1962; revisions through the present
  reflect changing market structure); R57's Evolution subsection
  covers this. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.286-290.
- The reader needs the Professional Conduct Program's procedural
  detail (complaint process, investigation rights, sanction tiers);
  the card states the disciplinary outcome space but not the
  procedural mechanism. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1
  Vol.6/pp.296-304.
