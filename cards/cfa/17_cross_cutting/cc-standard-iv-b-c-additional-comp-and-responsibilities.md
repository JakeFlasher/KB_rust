# Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements + IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors

## Intuition

Standard IV.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to not accept gifts,
benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or
might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with
their employer's interest, UNLESS they obtain written consent from
all parties involved. The Standard's anchor is the
employer-consent gate: side compensation arrangements that COULD
compete with employer interest are PERMITTED only when the employer
consents in writing after full disclosure. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-409.

Standard IV.C REQUIRES Members and Candidates who supervise others
in matters subject to the Code and Standards to take reasonable
steps to detect and prevent violations of applicable laws, rules,
regulations, and the Code and Standards by anyone subject to their
supervision or authority. The Standard imposes an affirmative
detect-and-prevent obligation, NOT a passive non-participation
obligation: a supervisor who knew or should have known about a
subordinate's violation but did not act is liable under IV.C even
absent direct participation in the violation. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.410-418.

## Definition

Standard IV.B REQUIRES Members and Candidates to not accept gifts,
benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or
might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with
their employer's interest, UNLESS they obtain written consent from
all parties involved. The "all parties involved" typically includes
the employer, the client (when the additional compensation comes
from the client), and the external compensator. **Source:** CFA
Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-409.

The IV.B violation predicate is the conjunction of (a) the
compensation could reasonably compete with employer interest AND
(b) the Member accepted it without written consent. Both must be
true; either alone is not a violation. Routine vendor gifts that
do not compete with employer service offerings are NOT IV.B
violations (though I.B independence may still apply). **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-409.

Standard IV.C REQUIRES Members and Candidates who supervise others
to take reasonable steps to detect and prevent violations. The
obligation is structured around three subactivities: (1) DETECT —
review subordinate work, monitor trading and communications, audit
performance; (2) PREVENT — provide compliance training, communicate
firm policies, restrict access to confidential information; (3)
DOCUMENT — maintain records of supervisory actions to enable
ex-post audit and demonstrate diligence. **Source:** CFA Institute
(2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.410-418.

The inadequate-compliance-system trigger (REQUIRES) captures
supervisors who took office in a firm whose compliance system is
inadequate but did NOT escalate or decline the supervisory role:
the Standard REQUIRES the supervisor either to decline the role
or to escalate the compliance defect to senior management. A
supervisor who silently inherits an inadequate compliance system
and then a subordinate violates is liable under IV.C even when the
supervisor had no specific knowledge of the violation. **Source:**
CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.413-418.

## Mathematical Reasoning

The IV.B written-consent gate (source REQUIRES) operationalizes the
agent-principal loyalty framework from IV.A: a Member who accepts
side compensation without employer consent is potentially serving
two principals (the employer and the side compensator) without
disclosure, which by definition compromises the agent's loyalty.
The written-consent requirement is what distinguishes IV.B
violations from routine professional courtesies: a fully disclosed
and consented arrangement is NOT a violation even if the side
compensation competes with employer interest; the failure to
disclose is the violation predicate, not the arrangement itself.
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-409.

The IV.C detect-prevent-document triad (source REQUIRES) builds an
affirmative-obligation supervision model: passive
non-participation in a subordinate's violation is insufficient; the
supervisor must AFFIRMATIVELY check for violations (DETECT),
AFFIRMATIVELY structure the environment to discourage violations
(PREVENT), and AFFIRMATIVELY document those efforts (DOCUMENT). The
absence of any of the three is itself a IV.C violation. The
Recommended Procedures (RECOMMENDS) include written compliance
manuals, periodic compliance training, mandatory reporting of
suspicious activity, and supervisor-specific compliance review
cycles. **Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.413-418.

The IV.B-vs-IV.C distinction (source ASSERTS) is structurally
important even though both Standards belong to the
Duties-to-Employers family: IV.B governs the Member's own
compensation (a vertical duty between Member and employer over the
Member's own outside arrangements); IV.C governs the Member's
oversight of subordinates (a horizontal duty between Member and
firm over the Member's supervisory obligations). The two Standards
do not overlap in fact patterns: a IV.B violation does NOT also
violate IV.C unless the Member is the supervisor of someone whose
violation the Member missed. The card body visually distinguishes
IV.B's vertical gate from IV.C's horizontal supervision tree to
make the distinction concrete (per Codex Round-2 directive).
**Source:** CFA Institute (2022) L1 Vol.6/pp.406-418.
