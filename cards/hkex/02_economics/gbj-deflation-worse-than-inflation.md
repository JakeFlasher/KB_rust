## Thesis

For an equity investor, deflation is far more dangerous than inflation — mild inflation solves many problems. Compounding must be measured in real (purchasing-power) terms: a true return is getting back more goods than you lent, not nominal money that buys fewer goods. He set realistic long-horizon expectations (even 10x-in-10-years is hard; ~20x-in-20 is good), revised down with experience. China stayed low-inflation despite global inflation due to factory overcapacity plus weak domestic demand.

## Deflation, Not Inflation, Is the Equity Investor's Danger

At the height of the mid-2022 global inflation scare he posted a one-line position: 「對股票投資者而言，通縮遠遠比通脹可怕，溫和的通脹可以解決很多麻煩事。」 — "For equity investors, deflation is far, far more frightening than inflation; mild inflation can solve a lot of troublesome problems." Two scope limits are built into his own sentence and should travel with the principle. First, it is framed for equity investors specifically (對股票投資者而言), not as a statement about households or society at large. Second, the inflation he is relaxed about is mild (溫和) inflation: the claim is that moderate price rises lubricate nominal revenues, debt burdens, and asset prices, not that inflation at any level is benign.

## Compounding Is Measured in Rice

His test for whether a return is real is a barter thought-experiment: 「複利是我借給你100擔大米，一年後你還我120擔大米，而不是我借你足夠買100擔大米的錢，一年后你還給我的錢只夠買80擔大米。」 — "Compounding is: I lend you 100 dan of rice and one year later you repay me 120 dan of rice. It is not: I lend you enough money to buy 100 dan of rice and one year later the money you repay only buys 80 dan." The unit of account for a long-horizon investor is goods, not currency; a nominal gain that buys back less rice than was lent is a loss in his accounting, which is exactly why deflation-versus-inflation matters to him at all. He applies the same realism to the rate at which compounding can plausibly run. In a separate exchange in the corpus (post 224291325) he wrote 「我以前也覺得十年十倍應該不難做到，這兩三年覺得如果二十年能夠有二十倍已經相當不錯」 — "I used to think 10x in ten years shouldn't be hard to achieve; over the last two or three years I've come to feel that 20x in twenty years would already be quite good." That is an explicit downward revision of his own target with experience: 20x over 20 years is roughly 16% a year, versus the ~26% a year that 10x-in-10 implies.

## Why China Stayed Low-Inflation in 2022

With global inflation severe and the renminbi weakening, he addressed the puzzle of why Chinese inflation would not ignite: 「我認為主要原因是工廠供應過剩+內需不振」 — "I think the main reason is factory oversupply plus weak domestic demand." Manufacturing overcapacity caps goods prices from the supply side while weak internal demand removes the pull from the demand side, so external inflation does not transmit. The thread drew heavy pushback from mainland commenters insisting that the food prices they saw daily were soaring; he answered not by retreating but by reciting the official statistics release for June 2022 (CPI up 2.5% year-on-year, PPI up 6.1%, both historical figures from that release), holding the low-CPI premise under challenge. Read together with the first section, the diagnosis is not a comfort: an oversupply-plus-weak-demand economy leans toward the deflationary side he considers the genuinely dangerous one for equity holders.
