## Thesis

Treat 'all operations normal' denials of negative rumors skeptically — companies have issued exactly such reassurances shortly before blowups (华融 cited). Be alert to engineered/promotional rallies where 'mysterious capital' pumps fundamentals/GMV to harvest the stock; the falsification test is whether traffic/GMV collapses next month.

## A strong denial removes no risk — stay 將信將疑

When a negative rumor circulated about a bank he followed (thread context places it on 南京银行), the author refused to resolve it in either direction: "我看了傳聞，將信將疑。想想當年$中国华融(02799)$ 也強調公司一切經營正常" — "I read the rumor and am half-believing, half-doubting (將信將疑). Remember how back then 中国华融 (02799) also stressed that all of the company's operations were normal." This is analogical reasoning from one precedent he personally recalls, not a general law he asserts: he goes on to quote, at length, Huarong's 2018 spokesman statement — issued right after its chairman came under investigation — insisting that operations were "一切正常" and entirely unaffected, the reassurance that preceded Huarong's blowup. The working stance is symmetric: the company's denial is not evidence of safety (he has watched the exact phrase fail before), and the rumor is not evidence of guilt. He holds the position at half-belief instead of letting the official statement close the question.

## Engineered rallies: a cynical hypothesis that ships with its own test

When 新东方在线's livestreaming pivot turned the stock white-hot within days, the author posted what he expressly labeled an 邪惡的想法 — an "evil thought," a cynical hypothesis, not an accusation: "會不會有神秘資金到處買流量免費輸送給新东方，甚至動員各方人馬直接去直播間採購，做大GMV，然後在股市上收割？" — "Could there be mysterious capital buying traffic everywhere and feeding it to New Oriental for free, even mobilizing all sides to go purchase directly in the livestream rooms, inflating GMV, and then harvesting in the stock market?" He escalated it only conditionally: if traffic or GMV were to fall sharply over the following month, then "this would no longer be just an evil thought." The reusable mechanics: a too-sudden rally riding promotional fundamentals (traffic, GMV) triggers a manipulation hypothesis, never a conclusion; and the hypothesis comes packaged with a falsification test — wait for next month's traffic/GMV print before believing either the pump or your own cynicism. In the same name he separately flagged an anomalous single-day turnover of roughly 100% of the float excluding major shareholders' stakes — he raised the anomaly itself as suspicious without asserting the harvest story, corroborating the inflated-volume leg of the pattern only.
