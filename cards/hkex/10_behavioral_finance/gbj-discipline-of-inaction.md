## Thesis

In a single high-engagement post (224442723), the author argues that the hardest thing in investing is to research a stock — or even an entire industry — fully and then do nothing; anyone who can research a whole industry and still hold back has the makings of a master-level investor. He sharpens this in the same thread: when a commenter notes 'if nothing meets the buy standard, you naturally won't buy,' he replies that the buy standard has to be set very strict (那符合標準要頂得很嚴才可以). Separately, and nearly four years later in an unrelated thread, he gives a one-line pass on a stock he was asked about ('我看過了，不感興趣' on 老凤祥). Supported core: deep research followed by deliberate inaction is a discipline, and the buy bar should be deliberately high so that no qualifying candidate means buying nothing. NOT supported by the cited quotes: any 'circle of interest' framework — the 'not interested' remark is an unexplained, offhand pass, not a stated screening philosophy. Conviction is stated-once for the core thesis; evidence is moderate but thin on the 'pass' subprinciple.

## Research fully, then dare to walk away

In a 2022-07-05 post that drew heavy engagement, the author states the principle in one stroke: 「在投資中最難做到的是什麼？我認為應該是當你研究完一只股票，甚至是一整個行業，最後卻什麼也不做。如果有人研究完一整個行業，卻能夠做到放手，應該有能力成為大師級投資者。」 — "What is the hardest thing to do in investing? I think it is when you have finished researching a stock — or even an entire industry — and in the end do nothing at all. If someone can research an entire industry and still manage to let go, they have the ability to become a master-level investor." The discipline is not about skipping the work: the research is carried to completion, and mastery shows in refusing to let the sunk effort talk you into a position. Readers in the thread immediately recognized the trap — one admitted that not buying feels like a betrayal of the effort already spent — which is precisely the pull the principle is built to resist.

## The buy bar must be held very strict

The mechanism that makes inaction sustainable surfaces in the same comment thread. A commenter (parabird) observes that if nothing meets the buy standard, you naturally won't buy; the author endorses and tightens that framing rather than originating it: 「那符合標準要頂得很嚴才可以」 — "then the qualifying standard has to be held very strict for that to work." The ordering matters. A loose bar lets completed research rationalize marginal entries, so "nothing qualifies" never happens; a strict bar makes "nothing qualifies, so buy nothing" the default output of the process rather than a repeated act of willpower. Note that the buy-standard vocabulary came from the commenter — the author's own contribution is the insistence that the bar be very strict.

## The terse pass — and its limits

Nearly four years later (2026-03), in a thread about supermarket-versus-egg-shop competition, a reader asks in passing whether he has looked at 老凤祥 (Lao Feng Xiang). His entire answer: 「我看過了，不感興趣」 — "I've looked at it; I'm not interested." Take this for exactly what it is: a one-line, rationale-free pass that is consistent with the temperament above, nothing more. He articulates no "circle of interest" or 能力圈-style doctrine in these threads, and this remark should not be read as a stated screening philosophy. The durable, supported content is the pairing in the 2022 post and its own comment thread: complete the research, hold the bar very high, and accept that doing nothing is often the correct result.
