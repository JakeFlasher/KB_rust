## Thesis

For a cost-plus manufacturer, falling raw-material costs compress gross profit (and trigger inventory write-downs), lowering gross margin rather than helping it. Distinguish gross-profit-spread widening from gross-margin widening when input costs rise. Watch 'revenue up, profit not up' (增收不增利) when cost inflation cannot be passed to customers (geography matters). For centralized procurement (集采), the margin hit can be muted if selling expenses fall in step — assess net margin, not headline price cuts.

## Cost-plus pricing inverts the input-cost intuition

In June 2022, rebutting the popular view that cheaper raw materials are good news for a contract manufacturer — he opened the post by calling that view nonsense — the author laid out the cost-plus mechanics: 「作為成本加成定價方式的制造廠，如果原料成本下跌只會令毛利下降，疊加庫存降價，毛利率也會是降的。」 — "For a factory that prices on a cost-plus basis, falling raw-material costs only push gross profit down; stacked with inventory markdowns, the gross-margin percentage falls too." The chain is mechanical: under cost-plus the selling price is the cost base plus a markup, so when the cost base shrinks, the absolute gross profit shrinks with it, while inventory bought at the old, higher prices must be marked down. Both the gross-profit dollars and the gross-margin percentage fall. The thread arose around textile maker 鲁泰 (Luthai), whose Vietnamese plant he knew first-hand through a friend's neighboring factory.

## Spread vs margin, and costs that cannot be passed on

The mirror case is input-cost inflation with only partial pass-through. Reporting from the 联邦制药 (United Laboratories, 3933) shareholder meeting — 6-APA selling prices were strong, but coal and corn costs had soared — he answered whether 6-APA had disappointed: 「毛利差還是擴大了，但是毛利率並沒有擴大。」 — "The gross-profit spread still widened, but the gross-margin percentage did not widen." Per-unit price-minus-cost can grow in absolute terms while the ratio stalls, because the price in the denominator rose alongside the spread. Read the two measures separately before extrapolating margin expansion from a rising spread.

When rising costs cannot be passed on at all, the result is what he repeatedly tagged 增收不增利 — "revenue up, profit not up": in the same weeks he flagged another manufacturer whose top line recovered without any profit growth, and he tied pass-through power to geography — Vietnamese fuel costs were rising far faster than China's, and the operator there could not push the increase onto its customers.

## 集采: weigh the headline cut against the expense line — and keep his hedge

On the insulin centralized-procurement round: 「胰島素藥集採雖然降價達50%，不過由於銷售支出也會同步下降，暫時看對毛利率和利潤都影響不大」 — "Although insulin 集采 cut prices by as much as 50%, because selling expenses also fall in step, for now it looks like little impact on gross margin and profit." Two cautions belong with this. First, it was an explicitly tentative read: 集采 had only started in May, the full-year outcome depended on peers' second-half bids, and he repeated in the comments that both 6-APA and insulin 集采 carried enough uncertainty that he would not judge the full year. Second, "assess net margin" is this card's analytic rendering, not his phrasing: strictly, lower selling expenses sit below the gross-profit line, so they cushion operating and net profit rather than gross margin itself — the author lumped 毛利率和利潤 (gross margin and profit) together loosely. The durable move is the same either way: model a procurement price cut net of the selling-expense relief that comes with it, instead of reacting to the headline percentage.
