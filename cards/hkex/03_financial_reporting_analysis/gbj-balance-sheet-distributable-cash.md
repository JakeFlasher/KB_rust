## Thesis

Reported cash overstates distributable cash: PRC subsidiaries paying up to an overseas/HK parent incur 10% withholding (100bn transferred yields 90bn, directly cutting profit), so cash-rich firms may not buy back/pay out the full balance. Heavy debt explains a small dividend — read low payout against the balance sheet. Check receivables/counterparty quality (bank-acceptance-bill receivables are low-risk; a fully provisioned bad debtor de-risks the line), and beware partners running high leverage. A first-ever special dividend with strong cash flow signals improved capital-return capacity.

## Cash on the Books Is Not Cash the Parent Can Spend

When a commenter demanded to know why a company with 270多億 of funds on its balance sheet would not spend HK$3bn buying back 100m shares, the author answered with repatriation mechanics rather than sentiment: 「賬上的錢在子公司。按照國稅法，向海外母公司派息，要交10%稅，如果子公司轉100億元往香港公司，只有90億元到賬，增加10億元稅項支出，直接影響利潤。」 — the money on the books sits in the mainland subsidiaries; under PRC tax law, paying dividends up to an overseas parent incurs a 10% withholding, so if a subsidiary transfers 100億 yuan to the Hong Kong company, only 90億 arrives, and the extra 10億 of tax expense hits profit directly. He offered this in defense of the firm's decision not to repurchase: the inference a reader should take is that a headline cash pile cannot be mobilized at face value, not that the tax forbids payout. Note carefully that this is the corporate-level withholding on intra-group dividends from a mainland subsidiary to its offshore parent — a separate tax from the 10% withholding individual investors face on H-share dividends.

## Reading Payout, Receivables, and Partners Against the Balance Sheet

Heavy debt explains a small dividend. Asked why 联邦制药 paid out so little, his entire reply was 「負債累累」 — "buried in debt." A low payout should be read against the liability side of the balance sheet before it is read as anything else.

On receivable quality, from his 联邦制药 (03933) shareholder-meeting notes: 「恒大的應收已經撥備，公司現在的應收都是銀行票據，風險較低，公司會加大風險管理。」 — the Evergrande receivable has already been provisioned; the company's current receivables are all bank acceptance bills, lower risk; and the company will step up risk management. The Evergrande exposure arose from a land sale (「前年廠地賣給恆大地產，尾數收不到」 — the year before last the factory land was sold to Evergrande Real Estate and the balance could not be collected), and he quantified the cleanup in the same thread: 「去年全部撥備了，三億多」 — fully provisioned last year, 300-odd million. The mechanics: a fully provisioned bad debtor no longer hangs over the receivables line, and bills receivable backed by bank acceptance carry bank credit rather than customer credit.

The counterparty corollary appears elsewhere in his corpus: 「和玩高槓桿游戲的河南人合作，我將來又要擔心應收賬風險了」 — partnering with a counterparty playing the high-leverage game means worrying about receivable risk all over again, written just after the Evergrande burn. A partner's leverage is a leading indicator of your own receivable problem.

Finally, the capital-return signal: 「公司今年是上市以來首次派特別股息，現在公司的現金流很好，和以前不一樣。」 — this year the company paid a special dividend for the first time since listing; its cash flow is now very good, unlike before. A first-ever special dividend backed by strong cash flow marks a genuine change in capacity to return capital.
