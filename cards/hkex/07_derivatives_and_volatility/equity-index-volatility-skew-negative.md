## What the source says

Colin Bennett's Trading Volatility notes that in the equity market the implied-volatility skew is negative — downside strikes trade at higher implied volatility than upside strikes — and contrasts this with the positive skew seen in some volatility products. Negative equity skew reflects persistent demand for downside protection.

## Why it matters for an option-income writer

Negative skew means out-of-the-money puts are systematically richer (higher implied vol) than equidistant calls. A seller of downside puts (cash-secured puts) therefore collects an embedded skew/insurance premium, while a buyer of protection pays it; understanding skew is what separates harvesting the put risk-premium from simply being short volatility.
