## Thesis

Avoid accumulating a large minority stake in an illiquid small-cap: the controlling shareholder can take it private at a steep discount, while the small holder's only edge is the ability to walk away — which you forfeit once the position is too big to exit (his own ~3% stake took three years to exit). He targets high-liquidity, positive-cash-flow 'junk' value names for a shareable book and deliberately keeps illiquid holdings private.

## The Asymmetry: Privatization Power vs. Exit Power

In a July 2022 post the author recounts having once bought nearly 3% of a small-cap's float (接近3%流通) and then needing three full years to exit the position completely. His conclusion: 「大股東的優勢在於它可以大折價私有化，小股東的優勢在於我們可以拍拍屁股就走人。真心勸喻這種傻事少做」 — "The controlling shareholder's advantage is that it can privatize at a steep discount; the small shareholder's advantage is that we can pat the dust off and walk away. I sincerely urge you to do less of this kind of foolish thing." The point is structural: in an illiquid name, the controlling holder owns the privatization option and can exercise it at a price far below fair value, while the minority holder's only countervailing power is liquidity — the ability to leave at will. A stake that is large relative to the float destroys exactly that power, leaving the holder with neither control nor exit. In the same post he names Hong Kong professional investor David Webb — who over the years became the second-largest shareholder of many small-caps — as the cautionary extreme, summing up Webb's predicament in one word: 慘 (grim).

## Screening the Shared Book; Trimming Even When the Income Is Fine

For the portfolio he shares publicly, liquidity is the first explicit screen, not an afterthought: 「以較高流動性噁心垃圾為目標，盡可能有點股息收/回購，正現金流入，大方向還能有點增長。」 — "Target higher-liquidity disgusting garbage; as far as possible with some dividend income / buybacks, positive cash inflow, and a general direction that still has some growth." The deliberately self-deprecating 噁心垃圾 ("disgusting garbage") is his label for deeply out-of-favor value names; the filter stack reads liquidity first, then cash returned to shareholders (dividend or buyback), positive cash flow, and modest growth. Low-liquidity holdings he considers unsuitable for sharing, so he keeps them out of the published book rather than out of his own.

The discipline binds even when the income on a small-cap is acceptable. Asked in a thread on Texwinca (德永佳) why he had heavily trimmed the position despite calling its full-year dividend decent, he answered: 「流動性原因，不想持有太多小票。」 — "Liquidity reasons; I don't want to hold too much in small-caps." An acceptable yield does not override the position-size-versus-float constraint: he caps small-cap exposure for liquidity even where the income story still works.
