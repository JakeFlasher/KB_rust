## Thesis
When the state is the controlling shareholder, a cash-needy government can simply raise the payout ratio rather than impose a windfall tax, since the dominant shareholder captures most of the dividend — a counter to windfall-tax fears. For high-payout state coal/power names, the bull case is the listed co sustaining super-high payout while the parent absorbs heavy new-energy capex; minority holders prefer dividends over the company itself building new energy. Catalyst for a catalyst-light SOE H-share is a payout-raising corporate action (e.g. A-share relisting).

## The payout-ratio lever vs the windfall-tax fear
In June 2022, with CNOOC (中国海洋石油 00883) earning windfall oil profits, a commenter worried that a cash-strapped government would very likely impose a special excess-profit levy. The author's counter was structural: 「政府缺錢只要加大派息力度就可以了，大股東持股比例不低。」 — "If the government is short of cash, it only needs to step up the dividend payout; the controlling shareholder's stake is not small." Because the state already owns the dominant stake, raising the payout ratio routes most of the windfall to the treasury through ordinary dividends — a cleaner extraction channel than a new tax, and one in which minority holders get paid alongside the state rather than expropriated.

Note what this argument is and is not. It was offered as a counter to a tax fear — the payout is the state's alternative way to collect — not as a prediction that no tax would ever be levied. In the same thread the author kept the levy alive in his own arithmetic, presenting his CNOOC profit estimate as computed 未考慮暴利稅 ("without considering a windfall tax"): the tax stays a real possibility he deliberately left out of the model, not one he ruled out.

## Where the new-energy capex sits decides the dividend
Three weeks later, when China Shenhua's H shares fell harder than CNOOC despite strong earnings and yield, the author diagnosed the structural worry: 「市場擔心的是$中国神华(01088)$ 將來會不會在上市公司層面直接上馬新能源項目。現在一直都是上市公司超高派息，然後再由母公司大額投資上馬新能源項目。」 — "What the market fears is whether Shenhua will, in future, launch new-energy projects directly at the listed-company level. The standing arrangement is that the listed company pays a super-high dividend, and the parent then makes the large new-energy investments." The check is mechanical: for a high-payout SOE, find which level the growth capex sits at. Parent-level capex supports the payout — and when a commenter spelled out the causality (the parent's heavy cash needs are precisely what make the super-high payout sustainable), the author endorsed it with 「就是這個邏輯」, "that is exactly the logic." Listed-co-level capex competes with the dividend, and that is what the H-share market punishes.

Asked why the market would not cheer Shenhua building new energy itself — doesn't the market love new energy? — his answer named the shareholder base: 「神華的小股東們更喜歡派息」 — "Shenhua's minority shareholders prefer the dividend payout." Holders of these names self-selected for cash yield; on-balance-sheet growth capex reads as value subtraction to them even when the sector it buys is fashionable.

## The payout action as the catalyst
For a catalyst-light SOE H-share — his example was Dongfeng Motor Group (东风集团股份 00489) — he saw essentially nothing to look forward to except a payout-raising corporate action: an A-share relisting that lifts the payout ratio. The same lever that answers the windfall-tax fear is also the realistic value catalyst when nothing else is on the calendar.
