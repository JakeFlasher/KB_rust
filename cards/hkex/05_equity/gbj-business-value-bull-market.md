## Thesis

He judges whether he's in a bull market by the business, not the quote: a portfolio whose per-share shareholder equity and dividends keep growing every year (~10% weighted, pre-ex-dividend) is fundamentally in a bull regime regardless of price action. Equities are a positive-expectancy game over a long enough horizon, provided you compress costs and avoid losing industries.

## The regime is defined by the business, not the tape

In June 2022, with his Hong Kong holdings deep in drawdown, he declared: 「權益資產一直都處於牛市階段，我的持股組合每年的股東權益和派息都持續增長，怎麼可能不是呢？股價，就別提了」 — "Equity assets have been in a bull-market stage all along. My holdings' shareholder equity and dividend payouts keep growing every year — how could it not be? The share price? Don't even bring it up." The regime test runs entirely on per-share business fundamentals: so long as the equity base and the cash distributions compound annually, the portfolio is in a bull market, whatever the quote screen says.

When a follower (陈嘉禾) pressed him for the actual number, he answered in a reply — not in the post body — 「加權計算10%上下，除息前」: "weighted, around 10%, before ex-dividend." Two bounds keep this figure honest. First, it traces to that follow-up reply rather than to the headline post, and it describes his portfolio as of mid-2022. Second, when the same follower suggested that reinvesting the dividends would lift the effective growth to 15-20%, he answered flatly 「沒有」 ("no") — the ~10% stands as stated, a weighted pre-ex-dividend equity growth rate that must not be inflated with reinvestment assumptions.

## An "average win" game — with two win conditions

The same morning he posted the underlying philosophy: 「股票是一個平均贏的游戲，前提是必須盡可能壓縮交易費用，以及避免輸家的行業。」 — "Stocks are an average-win game, on the precondition that you compress trading costs as much as possible, and avoid loser industries." Positive expectancy is the baseline property of equities, but only behind those two explicit conditions: minimize frictions, and stay out of structurally losing industries. Both conditions are his, stated verbatim in the post — the expectancy claim never appears without them.

The horizon qualifier also comes from a reply: when a skeptic asked whether mainland A-shares might be an exception (「大A股会不会特殊一点」), he answered 「時間不夠長而已」 — "the time simply hasn't been long enough." The average win is a long-horizon property; a market that looks like an exception is, in his reading, just one that hasn't been sampled long enough yet.
